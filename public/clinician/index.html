Method appendix

IntervalJeffreys, Beta(x+½, n−x+½), 90% credible
Why JeffreysWald intervals are badly wrong at small n and near 0% or 100% — exactly where a struggling phoneme sits — and can return a zero-width interval at 0/n.
Why 90%, not 95%At the sample sizes a home programme actually produces, a 95% interval is so wide it reads as "we know nothing", which is its own kind of misleading.
Minimum to report a rate8 attempts
Minimum to claim a direction20 attempts, and non-overlapping intervals, and ≥ 0.15 absolute change. Separated intervals alone admitted a false direction in 2.5% of simulated pure-noise learners; adding a minimum effect size was not cosmetic.
Measured false-positive rate3.0% over 200 simulated coin-flip learners
Excluded from all ratesTaps (the child produced no speech) and attempts resolved on the encouragement cap (the scene resolved regardless of what was heard). Both counts are disclosed above rather than silently dropped.
Known limitationScoring thresholds are calibrated on adult speech. Every figure inherits that limitation.
