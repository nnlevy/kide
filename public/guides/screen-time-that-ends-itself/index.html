Design

The screen time that ends without a fight

Almost every screen-time app ends the same way: a timer runs out, or you take the tablet away, and your child experiences the ending as something done to them. Pip ends differently — and that difference is the whole product, not a nice extra.

In Pip's world, the day runs west to east, from golden hour into dusk, while your child plays. Nobody announces a limit. Instead, as the light goes down, Pip gets sleepy — visibly, gradually, the same way a real toddler does at the end of a long afternoon — and eventually asks to be tucked in. Your child says goodnight. That's the session ending, and your child was the one who decided it was time.

Why the difference matters

A limit you enforce puts you and your child on opposite sides of the ending: you're the one taking something away, so the feeling that follows is loss. A limit your child helps bring about — even a gently engineered one — puts you on the same side of it. Pip getting sleepy isn't a trick to disguise a timer; it's a real narrative reason to stop that a toddler can understand and participate in, the same way "the sun is going down, it's bedtime" already works at home.

That's also why there's no countdown, no "5 more minutes" warning, and no visible clock anywhere in the game. A visible timer turns the whole session into something to race against. A sky that's just quietly getting darker doesn't.

What we deliberately left out

No streak to protect, no "come back tomorrow" nag, no paywall dressed up as a bedtime. If Pip's goodnight were gamified — a bonus for logging off on time, say — it would put the same performance pressure back on the ending that we're trying to remove from the potty routine and everywhere else. An ending you're rewarded for isn't really yours; see our guide on why Pip's routines aren't scored for the longer version of that argument.

What this doesn't replace

Pip's goodnight makes the handoff gentler — it doesn't set how much screen time is right for your child, and we're not going to pretend an app should decide that for you. The Parent Corner is where you're in control of what's on; the sleepy goodbye is just how we hand the moment back once you've decided it's time.
