The goodbye is the whole point

Near the end of a session the sky turns amber, then dusk, and Pip starts to yawn. Your child is the one who tucks Pip in. Nothing locks, nothing is taken away, and there is no timer to argue with — which is why handing the tablet back stops being a fight.

Then your child names them — and that name becomes the first word they practise, because a name you chose is the most motivating word there is.

The practice is invisible

Your child never sees a lesson. They see a companion who needs a rope, and a hill with somebody behind it. Underneath, every word carries a speech sound chosen for what your child is ready for next — so it never turns into a test.

Wondering which sounds your child should have by now? Most of the time the answer is “that one is still on its way, and that's on time.”

Pip talks. And, if you like, listens.

Turn listening on in the Parent Corner and your child can just say “red!” instead of tapping. We only ever listen on the device — nothing is recorded, stored, or sent, and where a browser would need to ship your child's voice to a company's servers, we don't offer the microphone at all.

Nothing here is scored

Going Potty and Washing Hands are pretend play, not quizzes. Your child helps Pip through the steps as many times as they want; there is no wrong tap and no way to fail. Pip sometimes doesn't make it in time, on purpose — so if it happens to your child, they have already watched it be okay.
