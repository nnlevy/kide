Your child picks who comes with them

Four companions, and the choice matters more than it looks. Each one gets stuck for a different reason, so the world reads differently depending on who your child brings. Then your child names them — and that name becomes the very first word they practise, because a name you chose is the most motivating word in the language.

Hundreds of little lessons, hiding in one afternoon

Your child never sees a lesson. They see a companion who needs a rope, and a hill with somebody behind it. Underneath, every word carries a speech sound, and every sound is chosen for what your child is ready for next — so the practice is invisible and never turns into a test.

Pick a sound to see the words that carry it.

Every option offered at any moment practises the same sound — so wherever your child chooses to go, there is no wrong door, and they can never quietly avoid the sound they find hardest.

Pip's Turn: rehearsing the routine

A toddler will happily play with a toy sink that has no water in it. Nothing is being rewarded — doing the routine is the whole reward, and being the one who does it is the point. Pip's Turn is built on that. Your child doesn't answer questions about the potty; they help Pip go, step by step, as many times as they want. Nothing is scored, there is no wrong tap, and there is no way to fail.

Pip sometimes sits and nothing happens. Pip sometimes doesn't make it in time. Both are deliberate, and nobody is cross about either — so that if it happens to your child, they have already watched it be okay. We never reward a child for going, because pressure is the fastest route to holding it in. And we'd rather you played this before the real moment, in another room: a screen on the toilet pulls attention away from exactly the signals your child is learning to notice.

Why it's built this way, with the research →

Pip talks. And, if you like, listens.

Every question is spoken aloud in Pip's voice, so a two-year-old who can't read a word can still play alone. Turn it on in the Parent Corner and Pip will listen too — your child can just say “red!” instead of tapping.

We only ever listen on the device. If a browser would need to send your child's voice to a company's servers to understand it, we don't offer the microphone there at all — there's no cloud option to switch on. Nothing is recorded, stored, or sent. Tapping always works, and listening is off until a grown-up turns it on.
